# Curriculum Peer Review Policy

**Status**: Draft
**Version**: 0.2
**Last Updated**: November 2, 2024
**Owner**: Curriculum Development Committee

## Purpose

This policy establishes a systematic peer review process for new courses and major curriculum revisions to ensure high-quality, consistent educational offerings that meet institutional standards and student needs.

## Scope

**Applies to**: 
- All new course proposals
- Major revisions to existing courses (>50% content change)
- New program proposals
- Significant changes to program requirements

**Does not apply to**: 
- Minor course updates (syllabus tweaks, textbook changes)
- Emergency course modifications
- One-time special topics courses (subject to simplified review)

## Policy Statement

All new courses and major curriculum changes must undergo peer review by at least two qualified reviewers before receiving approval from the curriculum committee. The review process should be collaborative, constructive, and completed within a reasonable timeframe to support curricular innovation while maintaining quality standards.

## Procedures

### Submitting a Proposal for Review

**Steps**:
1. Complete the appropriate proposal template (course or program)
2. Submit proposal as a GitHub issue with the "proposal" label
3. Tag the Curriculum Committee chair (@curriculum-chair)
4. Include any supporting documents in the `/docs/proposals/` folder

**Responsible Party**: Course/program proposer

### Assignment of Reviewers

**Steps**:
1. Curriculum Committee chair assigns two reviewers within 5 business days
2. Reviewers should have relevant expertise and no conflicts of interest
3. Reviewers are tagged in the proposal issue

**Responsible Party**: Curriculum Committee chair

### Conducting the Review

**Steps**:
1. Reviewers examine the proposal within 10 business days
2. Reviewers provide feedback by commenting on the GitHub issue
3. Reviewers use the peer review checklist (see below)
4. If significant concerns exist, reviewers may request revisions
5. Once satisfied, reviewers approve by commenting "LGTM" (Looks Good To Me)

**Responsible Party**: Assigned reviewers

### Peer Review Checklist

Reviewers should address:
- [ ] Are learning outcomes clear, measurable, and appropriate for the level?
- [ ] Does the course content align with stated outcomes?
- [ ] Are assessment methods appropriate and aligned with outcomes?
- [ ] Are prerequisites reasonable and clearly stated?
- [ ] Does the course fill a genuine need in the curriculum?
- [ ] Are required resources feasible and available?
- [ ] Does the proposal address equity and inclusion considerations?
- [ ] Is the workload reasonable for the credit hours?
- [ ] Are there any overlaps or conflicts with existing courses?

### Revisions and Resubmission

**Steps**:
1. Proposer addresses reviewer feedback
2. Proposer updates the proposal document and comments in the issue
3. Reviewers verify changes and provide final approval

**Responsible Party**: Proposer and reviewers

### Final Approval

**Steps**:
1. Once both reviewers approve, the Curriculum Committee chair schedules final vote
2. Committee votes in regular meeting (requires majority approval)
3. Decision is recorded in meeting notes and the issue is closed

**Responsible Party**: Curriculum Committee

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|-----------------|
| Proposer | Prepare complete proposal, respond to feedback, revise as needed |
| Reviewers | Provide timely, constructive feedback; approve when criteria are met |
| Curriculum Committee Chair | Assign reviewers, facilitate process, schedule final vote |
| Curriculum Committee | Final approval decision, ensure policy compliance |
