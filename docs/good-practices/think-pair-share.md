# Think-Pair-Share for Deeper Discussion

**Category**: Pedagogy / Student Engagement
**Shared by**: Maria Chen
**Date**: October 28, 2024
**Context**: Introduction to Educational Psychology (200-level, 35 students)

## Overview

Think-Pair-Share is a collaborative learning strategy where students first think individually about a question, then discuss with a partner, and finally share insights with the larger class. I implemented this technique to increase participation and deepen understanding of complex psychological theories.

## The Problem

In my Educational Psychology course, I noticed that:
- The same 5-6 students dominated class discussions
- Many students seemed hesitant to share ideas in front of the whole class
- Complex theories needed more processing time than I was allowing
- Some students would disengage during lecture-heavy segments

I wanted to create a more inclusive discussion environment where all students could develop and articulate their understanding before sharing with the larger group.

## The Solution

I restructured my class discussions to use a consistent Think-Pair-Share format for key concepts and case studies.

### Implementation Details

**What was done**:
- Identified 3-4 key discussion points per class session
- Posed open-ended questions requiring application or analysis
- Built in structured time for each phase:
  - Think (2-3 minutes): Students write notes individually
  - Pair (5-7 minutes): Students discuss with a partner
  - Share (10-15 minutes): Volunteer pairs share insights with class
- Used a visible timer to keep phases on track
- Encouraged note-taking during the share phase
- Sometimes asked pairs to write their insights on sticky notes to collect common themes

**Resources needed**:
- Timer (I used a simple online timer projected on screen)
- Sticky notes or index cards (optional, for collecting responses)
- Clear discussion questions prepared in advance

**Time investment**:
- Preparation: 30 minutes per class to develop quality questions
- Implementation: 20-25 minutes per discussion (vs. 15 minutes for traditional discussion)

## Results

**What worked well**:
- **Increased participation**: 90% of students actively engaged (vs. ~30% before)
- **Higher quality responses**: The thinking and pairing time led to more thoughtful contributions
- **Reduced anxiety**: Students felt more confident after testing ideas with a partner
- **Accountability**: The pair discussion ensured students couldn't "hide" in the crowd
- **Diverse perspectives**: Heard from students who previously never spoke up
- **Deeper understanding**: Students made more connections between theory and practice

**Challenges encountered**:
- **Time management**: Had to cut some lecture content to make room (solved by moving basic content to pre-class readings)
- **Odd numbers**: With 35 students, occasionally had a group of three (worked fine, just noted it)
- **Off-task conversations**: A few pairs needed redirection (addressed by walking around during pair phase)
- **Some students preferred working alone**: Made it optional for them to share with partner but still required individual thinking time

**Student feedback**:
End-of-semester survey showed:
- 85% said Think-Pair-Share helped them understand concepts better
- 92% appreciated the thinking time before having to speak
- 78% felt more comfortable participating in class
- Several students commented: "I actually looked forward to discussions instead of dreading them"

**Evidence of success**:
- Exam scores on application questions improved by an average of 12% compared to previous semester
- Observation notes showed more students speaking in class (averaged 15 different speakers per session vs. 6 previously)
- Class attendance remained high even on discussion-heavy days

## Recommendations

**Would you use this again?**: Absolutely yes! This has become my default discussion format.

**Advice for others**:
- **Craft questions carefully**: Avoid simple recall questions; use questions that require analysis, application, or evaluation
- **Set clear expectations**: On the first day, explain the format and why you're using it
- **Vary pair assignments**: Mix it up between choosing partners, assigned pairs, and random pairs
- **Model good discussions**: Early in semester, have a sample pair share their conversation process
- **Be okay with noise**: The room will be buzzing during pair time—that's productive chaos
- **Use a timer and stick to it**: This keeps energy up and ensures you get to the share phase
- **Honor the "think" time**: Don't let students skip straight to pairing—individual processing is crucial

**Adaptations for different contexts**:
- **Large lectures (100+ students)**: Use this for breakout groups of 4-6 instead of pairs, or implement in discussion sections
- **Online/hybrid**: Use breakout rooms for the pair phase, then return to main room for sharing
- **Introductory courses**: Provide more structure (sentence starters, specific roles for partners)
- **Advanced seminars**: Extend pair time and ask pairs to build on each other's ideas
- **Short class periods**: Use "Turn and Talk" (1-2 minutes per phase) as a quicker version
- **Assessment focus**: Collect the paired notes as formative assessment to guide future instruction
